# Community needs

*Focused scan, November 2026. Sources at the bottom.*

## What the assessments agree on

Across recent community needs assessments, the same five needs show up at
the top in different orders: **housing, utilities, food, transportation,**
and **childcare**. Jobs with adequate wages and mental-health support follow
close behind.

- Indiana community action agencies (2026 assessments): utility affordability
  now ranks #1 in several counties, trading places with food assistance from
  2024 — both stay in every top five, alongside housing, transportation, and
  childcare.
- ADVOCAP's 2025 assessment (Fond du Lac, Green Lake, Winnebago counties):
  housing instability 68%, transportation 65%, food insecurity 59%. The
  details matter — help paying utilities (50%), car repairs and fuel (44%
  each), monthly food support.
- Novant Health's 2024 social-needs screenings: patients' top needs were
  housing, food, and transportation, screened across food, housing, safety,
  transportation, and utilities.

The pattern: nobody has just one need. A neighbor who can't afford groceries
often also can't afford the car repair to reach a cheaper store. Strongo's
service list should reflect that overlap instead of treating food in
isolation.

## How Strongo maps to each need

| Need | What neighbors ask for | Strongo service idea | Priority |
|------|------------------------|----------------------|----------|
| Food | Monthly food support, affordable meals | Community-run delivery, bulk buying clubs, meal trains | First |
| Utilities | Help paying bills, weatherization | Aid-program navigation, home repair help | Third |
| Transportation | Car repairs, fuel costs, rides | Volunteer rides, repair pools, fuel sharing | Third |
| Housing | Rent help, repairs | Repair help; rent aid is out of scope for now | Partial |
| Childcare | Affordable, accessible care | Sitter co-op board (later phase) | Later |
| Tools / yard work | Chores, upkeep help | Shared tool library, chore board | Second |
| Good jobs | Wages, benefits | Driver and coordinator stipends that stay local | Ongoing |

## Why food goes first

1. **Frequency.** People eat every day, so a food service gets repeated use
   and fast feedback.
2. **Clear villain.** Delivery-app commissions of 15–30% give the community
   version an obvious price and fairness edge (see
   [food delivery](./food-delivery)).
3. **Proven models.** Bulk buying clubs, co-op box deliveries, community
   fridges, and volunteer driver networks already work — see KWT Mutual Aid's
   cooperative food project and the co-op box-delivery research below.
4. **Care path.** Meal trains and free-meal funds turn the same rails into
   direct care for people in need.

Tools and yard work come second because they need almost no regulation or
insurance relative to rides or childcare, and a tool library is cheap to
start. Transport coordination and utility-aid navigation follow once the
community trusts the platform with food.

## Open questions

- Which neighborhood pilots first, and who coordinates it?
- What does the community already run (fridges, buying clubs, meal trains)
  that Strongo should join rather than replace?
- How are free meals funded at the start — donations, surplus, grants?
